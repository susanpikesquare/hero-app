## 0. Foundational frame (from Erica's June 3 working session)

These are the words and the worldview the whole product is built on.
They are not features — they are the prior every decision tests against.

**The keywords.** Harmony · Peace · Joy. If a feature does not move a
family toward at least one of those three, it does not ship.

**A paradigm shift in parenting.** Most parenting tooling treats the
parent's job as a management task to be optimized. Home Hero treats it
as a *relationship* to be protected. The product takes the management
load so the relationship gets the attention.

**The Hilio Plow problem.** Erica's term for the modern parent: helicopter
parent meets snowplow parent. *"We are so afraid of our kids failing we
are setting them up to fail."* Kids need real chances to succeed and to
fail in the safety of their own homes. But homes are less emotionally
safe than they used to be — parents are stressed, tapped out, with no
patience left. **Home Hero exists to put back the safety, so the failing
and the succeeding can both happen at home.**

**Performative parenting vs. presence.** Many parents are *doing
things* with their kids but not *being a family* with them. They are in
reaction-management mode, never in let-me-just-be-with-you mode. Home
Hero exists to create the **mental and physical space** for the parent
to drop the management mode. The app takes the daily task management so
the parent can spend their attention on the things that are actually
meaningful.

**The AI's role: an unbiased resource.** *"AI won't hit limits but
people do."* A parent's patience runs out at 7pm; the AI's doesn't. The
AI carries the parts of the load that the parent's nervous system
should not have to carry — the reminders, the standard-keeping, the
specific feedback.

**Less is more.** Especially on the kid surface. A picture of "make
your bed" with no more than three short bullets. The kid surface is
ruthlessly minimal. App effectiveness *as an ally* — not as a
feature-stuffed toolbox.

## 0.5. What Home Hero is *not* (scope clarity)

Drawn directly from the June 3 session — say what we are not, so
everyone knows what we are.

**Not clinical, not therapy.** Nothing in the product references this
as clinical work, therapeutic advice, or professional expertise. Erica
is a clinical consultant who shapes the framework. The app is a
*family operating system*, not a clinical intervention.

**Not an ADHD app.** Home Hero serves every kid. Neurotypical,
neurodivergent, somewhere in between. The product surface is universal;
neurodivergence becomes optional parent-provided *context* (see
PRD §8A), never a label the child ever sees.

**Not a "how to parent your ADHD child" app.** We do not teach parenting
methodology. We do not run curricula. The product builds *executive
function support* and *reduces day-to-day inter-familial conflict* —
that is the whole job.

**Not a substitute for professional support.** When a parent is
genuinely struggling, the product gently surfaces a path to a
pediatrician or mental health provider. We refer; we do not treat.

## 1. Why this product exists

Home Hero is not a chore app. Chores are the surface — the thing on screen.
What we're actually doing is **redistributing the invisible labor of being
the household standard**, so the parent stops carrying it alone and the
kid gets to experience their own competence.

Underneath the photos and the tips and the AI feedback is a single
hypothesis: **most household conflict is not a discipline problem — it's
a clarity problem, an executive-function problem, and a feedback-loop
problem.** When everyone knows what "done" looks like, when the kid has
scaffolding to get there, and when the feedback is encouragement-first
and specific, the conflict that used to live in the relationship leaves
the relationship.

## 2. Problems we're solving

From today's brainstorm, clustered into themes:

### Connection (the relationship is the product)
- **Create connection** between parent and child
- **Reduce conflict** between parent/child and between spouses
- Move feedback **from the parent's body to a neutral surface** — the AI
  carries the "you missed a spot," so the parent can carry the "I see how
  hard you worked"

### Competence (the kid experiences themselves as capable)
- **Empower kids, eliminate entitlement** — by giving them real, specific
  recognition of effort and skill, not generic praise
- **Build habits** that the kid owns, not habits the parent enforces
- Answer the question every kid is silently asking: **"What is it I'm
  supposed to do better?"**

### Calm (everyone's nervous system gets to rest)
- **Reduce anxiety** — for kids ("I don't know the expectation") and for
  parents ("am I over-parenting or under-parenting?")
- **Remove reactivity, eliminate explosiveness** — by lowering the moments
  where the parent has to be the standard *in their voice*
- **Eliminate inconsistencies, create coherence** — the standard doesn't
  shift with the parent's mood

### Control (positive locus, not negative)
- Establish a **new locus of feedback control** — historically experienced
  by the kid as negative-from-the-parent; we redirect it to a net-new
  *positive* feedback loop
- Give parents back **confidence and context** — the trial-and-error of
  parenting becomes informed-by-clinical-standards parenting

## 3. Current state → future state

We mapped the parent today. Kid and family-system are proposed — to
confirm or revise with Erica tomorrow.

### The parent

**Current:** Overwhelmed, under-resourced, frustrated. Afraid they're
either over- or under-parenting and missing the mark. Emotionally
under-resourced, never enough time, always too much to be done. Don't
know what the right thing is to do. Learning through trial and error.

**Future:** Confident, contextualized, in a positive locus of control.
Has a shared standard with their kid that doesn't live in their voice.
Has clinical guidance available without scheduling a session. Has the
nervous-system bandwidth to be present for the parts of parenting that
actually require them.

### The kid *(draft — confirm with Erica)*

**Current:** Feels checked, monitored, criticized. Doesn't reliably know
what's expected. Reads parent's tone as the standard — and the standard
shifts. Chores feel like control. Doesn't get to experience competence —
because the feedback is "you forgot," not "you nailed step 1 and 2."

**Future:** Knows what "done" looks like — a picture, three tips, one
clear next step. Experiences competence through specific recognition
("Sheet flat? ✓ Pillow on top? ✓"). Has agency in *the choosing* —
which chore, when, in what order. Feels emotionally safe — the AI is
neutral, the parent's override is gentle, the failure mode is "try once
more, you've got this," not shame.

### The family system *(updated June 3)*

**Current:** One person — usually one parent — is the manager, the
controller, the regulator, the corrector. The household runs on their
nervous system. Spouses fight about whose standard is right. Siblings
feel measured against each other. Everyone is regulating outward instead
of being regulated inward.

**Future:** The standard is on the app, not in anyone's voice. The
parent is free to be the warmth and the witness. Spouses argue less
because they're aligned around a shared external reference. Siblings
each have their own developmental track, not a sibling comparison.

**Multi-household reality (new, from June 3).** A meaningful share of
the families we serve are split across two physical households —
separated parents, shared custody, blended families. For these kids,
the *standard shifting between houses* is a primary source of
confusion and conflict. Home Hero must support **one kid spanning
multiple households**, so the reference photo, the tips, and the
"what done looks like" stay consistent regardless of which parent's
roof the kid is under tonight. This creates *a level playing field
for the parents* too — neither one is "the strict one."

## 4. The age-differentiated experience

The product surface should feel meaningfully different at age 5, 10, and
17. We already have five age bands in the developmental matrix (4–5,
6–8, 9–12, 13–15, 16–18). Here's a proposed shape for each — to refine
with Erica.

### 4–5 (Pre-K / Kindergarten)
- **Kid surface:** barely a kid surface. App is parent-mediated.
- **Chores:** micro-tasks ("put shoes on the rack," "find your cup").
- **Coaching voice:** spoken by parent, not read by kid.
- **Success state:** "with grown-up," not "by self."
- **Parent's job:** scaffold the habit by being there, every time.

### 6–8 (early elementary)
- **Kid surface:** big buttons, reading-emerging, picture-heavy.
- **Chores:** stand-alone but with strong scaffolding (3 tips + reference photo).
- **Self-care emerging:** brush teeth solo, dress self.
- **Identity:** "hero" framing lands hardest here.
- **Parent's job:** witness specifically, override warmly.

### 9–12 (late elementary / tween) — *v0's sweet spot*
- **Kid surface:** current build is tuned for this band.
- **Chores:** menu of options, picks own order.
- **Risk:** kid feels surveilled — frame opt-in clearly.
- **Override messages from parent:** land with full weight.

### 13–15 (early teen)
- **Kid surface:** *needs a voice shift.* "Hero" + bunny may read as
  babyish. Consider a teen mode — same scaffolding, peer/coach voice,
  less mascot.
- **Chores:** household-shared (dishes, laundry, pickup of siblings) +
  starting on personal responsibilities (own laundry, own room as their domain).
- **Tension:** wants autonomy *and* validation. Big design challenge.
- **Parent's job:** less direction, more witnessing.

### 16–18 (older teen)
- **Kid surface:** barely a chore app at this band. More like a
  co-piloted life-skills tracker. Closer to "shared family operating
  system" than "scoreboard."
- **Chores:** household contribution + life-skill rehearsal (cooking,
  laundry, simple money, scheduling).
- **The clock:** the "days until launch" framing hits hardest here — for
  both kid and parent. Use carefully (see §5).
- **Parent's job:** consultant, not manager.

## 4¾. Locked in v1 from the June 3 session

These were on the triage list. The June 3 working session (and Erica's
in-progress workbook answers) moved them to confirmed. The PRD has the
engineering shape; this section captures the strategic intent so
nothing gets watered down in the build.

### Locked — Neurodivergence as parent-provided context

**Decided:** A single optional general flag at setup
(`neurotypical` / `neurodivergent` / `not specified`). Parent-facing
only. Never shown to the child. Seeds higher support defaults and
surfaces context-relevant coaching first.

**Why this lands here, not in the kid surface:** Per the June 3 voice
shift, Home Hero is *not* an ADHD app. It is a universal product. The
neurodivergence context exists so a parent who is dealing with the
specific reality of a neurodivergent kid *feels seen* in setup — and
gets defaults that match. It never reaches the child as a label.

**PRD anchor:** §8A, Workbook §5.4–5.6.

### Locked — Multi-household consistency

**Decided:** A single kid can be linked to multiple parent households
so the standard travels with the kid, not with the house.

**Why:** A kid bouncing between two homes already manages two sets of
expectations. Home Hero must reduce that load, not add to it. The
reference photo for "Make your bed" is the *same photo* whether the
kid is at Mom's or Dad's tonight.

**Open questions:**
- Multi-household relationship model: how is the second parent invited
  (per-kid invite by primary parent? mutual handshake?)
- What does each parent see of the other? (Probably: only the standards
  + completions, not each other's tone or notifications.)
- Conflict resolution when parents disagree on a standard. (Probably:
  per-household override on the standard, with a soft note that it
  diverges.)

### Locked — Notifications with built-in nudge awareness

**Decided:** The notification system is part of MVP, not a Beta nice-to-have.

**The shape:**
- **Kid reminders, gentle:** Morning (before school) and afternoon
  (when the kid is home from school) for today's required tasks.
  Local notifications in MVP; server push in Beta (per PRD §9.13).
- **Parent celebrations:** Parent is notified when a kid completes a
  task. The notification *carries the win*, not "your kid checked a
  box" — *"Alice made her bed. 8:14am."*
- **Parent gentle nudge:** A button on each child's row in the
  dashboard. Sends a soft, kid-voice reminder ("Hey Alice — quick
  reset, trash to the curb"). Replaces the in-person nag.
- **AI metacognition on nudges (Erica's idea):** If a parent has
  nudged the same kid 3+ times in a day, the AI surfaces a gentle
  prompt to the parent: *"You've nudged Alice 3 times today. Want to
  try a different approach? Here's one."* This is the framework's
  "encouragement first, even for the parent" rule applied to the
  parent's own behavior.

**Why this lands now:** "Reduced parental reminding" is one of the
three north-star MVP outcomes (PRD §3). Without the notification system,
that outcome is unmeasurable.

### Locked — Step 0: Welcome / Arrival (pre-setup)

**Decided:** Before the parent adds a kid, before they pick a chore,
they arrive. Step 0 is a brief welcome moment that meets them where
they are.

**Shape (Erica's words paraphrased):**
- A short educational moment — possibly a recording, possibly a
  paragraph.
- Lead with the parent's actual feeling: *"Endlessly frustrated,
  exhausted, and distanced from those you love most."* Name it. Let
  them be seen.
- Frame what comes next: the app is going to take some of this load.

**Why this lands now:** Per Erica's workbook Q2.3, the failure mode is
*"another thing to manage."* The Step 0 frame inoculates against that
— the parent enters knowing what the app is *for them*, not just what
it asks of them.

### Locked — Pre-signup self-assessment

**Decided:** Before signup, a short "is this for you" assessment:
- *Am I experiencing these things?* (list of the felt-experience items
  from §0)
- *Do I want these things?* (list of the keywords — harmony, peace, joy
  — plus specific outcomes)

**Why this lands now:** The assessment qualifies the audience (so we
don't onboard families the product can't help) and reframes the parent's
arrival from consumer to participant. It also seeds the analytics — we
know going in what each parent said they wanted.

**Open question:** Does the assessment gate access (you only get an
invite if you score "yes" enough), or is it purely a mirror? Susan's
instinct: purely a mirror.

### Locked (reframed) — Days-until-launch, as a presence framing

**Originally proposed as:** A countdown — "you have 3,285 days until
Alice launches." Erica's concern (correctly): a daily ticker becomes a
pressure machine for an already-anxious parent.

**Reframed (June 3):** The frame is *not urgency* — it's *presence vs.
performative*. The message:
> *"You are not behind. You are not failing. You are just very tired,
> and your kid still needs you to be the one who is just here. Home
> Hero is built so the daily managing stops eating the moments. Use
> the time we save you on being a family, not running one."*

**Surfaces (proposed):**
- A one-time onboarding moment (Step 0 territory) that names the years
  remaining without weaponizing them.
- A per-age-band reframe at 16 → "from building habits to rehearsing
  the launch."
- **Not** a daily ticker. Erica's veto holds.

### Locked — Weekly snapshot for overwhelm / over-scheduling

**Decided:** A weekly view that helps the family see the *total load*
across the week — not just the day. The goal: catch over-committing
and over-scheduling before it becomes the source of the stress the
product is trying to lift.

**Shape:**
- One screen, per kid, showing the week's tasks at a glance.
- Flags when the week's task count exceeds the age-appropriate ceiling
  (soft warning, per Workbook Q3.3).
- "Lighten the week" suggestion: pick 2-3 tasks to defer or drop.

**Phase:** Beta (post-MVP). MVP ships the data; this is the
visualization layer on top.

## 5. Open product concepts still in triage

The original brainstorm had eight concepts. D and the 13-15 voice shift
graduated to §4½. Neurodivergence-as-context, multi-household,
notifications, Step 0, the days-until-launch reframe, and weekly
snapshot all graduated to §4¾ on June 3. These four remain to decide
together.

### "There are no bad kids" — as a foundational assumption
- **What:** Make this explicit in the philosophy doc, alongside the 10
  Core Operating Principles.
- **Why:** It's the *prior* the whole product runs on. Already implied
  by "Assume the child is under-skilled, never unwilling." Naming it
  more directly may help.
- **Decision:** Add verbatim to `product-philosophy.md`? **Susan: yes,
  recommend.**

### Show what's developmentally happening — coherent picture *(reframed June 3)*
- **What:** Onboarding moment + ongoing article series. "Here's what
  your kid's brain is doing when they 'forget.'"
- **Why:** Reduces shame for everyone. Reframes failure as physiology.
- **Reframing note:** The earlier version of this concept was called
  "Show the ADHD mind." June 3 positioning is *not ADHD-specific*. The
  reframe: when the parent has set neurodivergence context, the
  content surfaces neurodivergence-aware framings first. When they
  haven't, it surfaces general developmental framings (Workbook Q3.1
  cited published sources, e.g. CDC, AAP). The mechanism is the same;
  the framing is universal.
- **Surfaces:**
  - First-run onboarding ("Before you set up chores, here's what we
    want you to know about your kid's brain at this age")
  - Per-age band article ("How a 9-year-old experiences chores")
  - Possibly: in-context insight when a kid's pattern shows up in data

### Define neurophysiology — full family reset
- **What:** A "reset" button for when things have spiraled — for parent
  AND for kid.
- **Why:** Erica's specialty. The product becomes a co-regulation tool,
  not just a chore tool.
- **Sketch:** 90-second guided flow — pause, breathe, name what's
  happening, reconnect. Different scripts per side (parent / kid).
- **Open question:** Where does it live? Always-available button vs.
  surfaced when the system detects a spiral?

### In-the-moment coaching ("stop and take 5 breaths")
- **What:** A 30-second beat before a charged action.
- **Surfaces:**
  - Parent: before finalizing an override, "Take a breath?" prompt
  - Kid: "Stuck?" button on a chore tile → guided regulation
- **Light scope.** ~1 week if Erica writes the scripts.

### Screen time as an earned reward *(from May 26 message)*
- **What:** New `reward_mode` option in Family Settings: track minutes
  of screen time earned as chores complete.
- **Why:** Erica asked. Most parents already informally tie chores to
  screen time; making it explicit + clinically-toned helps.
- **Open questions for Erica:**
  - Per-chore minutes, or daily pool ("today's chores done = 30 min
    unlocked")?
  - Language: "earned" or "unlocked"?
  - Override flow: does "Good enough for today" still grant full credit?
    (Recommend: yes.)
  - Per-family opt-in via reward_mode (matches existing bunny hops /
    badges) — confirm?
