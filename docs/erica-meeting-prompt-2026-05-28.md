# Home Hero — Product Vision & Working Framework

A living doc. Started in the Susan + Erica session on 2026-05-27.
Brings together the problems we're trying to solve, the parent and
kid we're building for, and the open questions still on the table.
This is the doc we work through and keep working through — it outlives
any single meeting.

## 1. Why this product exists

Home Hero is not a chore app. Chores are the surface. What we're
actually doing is redistributing the invisible labor of being the
household standard, so the parent stops carrying it alone and the kid
gets to experience their own competence.

Underneath the photos and the tips and the AI feedback is a single
hypothesis: most household conflict in ADHD homes is not a discipline
problem. It's a clarity problem, an executive-function problem, and a
feedback-loop problem. When everyone knows what "done" looks like,
when the kid has scaffolding to get there, and when the feedback is
encouragement-first and specific, the conflict that used to live in
the relationship leaves the relationship.

## 2. Problems we're solving (clustered)

CONNECTION (the relationship is the product)
- Create connection between parent and child
- Reduce conflict between parent/child and between spouses
- Move feedback from the parent's body to a neutral surface

COMPETENCE (the kid experiences themselves as capable)
- Empower kids, eliminate entitlement, by giving real specific
  recognition of effort and skill, not generic praise
- Build habits that the kid owns, not habits the parent enforces
- Answer the question every kid is silently asking: "what is it I'm
  supposed to do better?"

CALM (everyone's nervous system gets to rest)
- Reduce anxiety: kids ("I don't know the expectation") and parents
  ("am I over-parenting or under-parenting?")
- Remove reactivity, eliminate explosiveness, by lowering the moments
  where the parent has to be the standard in their voice
- Eliminate inconsistencies, create coherence: the standard doesn't
  shift with the parent's mood

CONTROL (positive locus, not negative)
- Establish a new locus of feedback control, redirected from
  "negative from the parent" to a net-new positive feedback loop
- Give parents back confidence and context

## 3. Current → future state

PARENT (locked from the May 27 session):
- Current: overwhelmed, under-resourced, frustrated, afraid of over-
  or under-parenting and missing the mark. Emotionally
  under-resourced, never enough time, always too much to be done.
  Don't know what the right thing is. Learning through trial and
  error.
- Future: confident, contextualized, in a positive locus of control.
  Has a shared standard with their kid that doesn't live in their
  voice. Has clinical guidance available without scheduling a
  session. Has the nervous-system bandwidth to be present for the
  parts of parenting that actually require them.

KID (draft, confirm with Erica):
- Current: feels checked, monitored, criticized. Doesn't reliably
  know what's expected. Reads parent's tone as the standard, and the
  standard shifts. Chores feel like control. Doesn't get to
  experience competence because the feedback is "you forgot," not
  "you nailed step 1 and 2."
- Future: knows what "done" looks like: a picture, three tips, one
  clear next step. Experiences competence through specific
  recognition. Has agency in the choosing: which chore, when, in
  what order. Feels emotionally safe.

FAMILY SYSTEM (draft, confirm with Erica):
- Current: one person, usually one parent, is the manager,
  controller, regulator, corrector. The household runs on their
  nervous system. Spouses fight about whose standard is right.
  Siblings feel measured against each other.
- Future: the standard is on the app, not in anyone's voice. The
  parent is free to be the warmth and the witness. Spouses argue
  less because they're aligned around a shared external reference.
  Siblings each have their own developmental track.

## 4. Age-differentiated experience

The product surface should feel meaningfully different at 5, 10, and
17. Five age bands matching Erica's developmental matrix.

4-5 (Pre-K / Kindergarten)
- Kid surface: barely a kid surface. App is parent-mediated.
- Chores: micro-tasks (put shoes on the rack, find your cup).
- Coaching voice: spoken by parent, not read by kid.
- Success state: "with grown-up," not "by self."
- Parent's job: scaffold the habit by being there, every time.

6-8 (early elementary)
- Kid surface: big buttons, reading-emerging, picture-heavy.
- Chores: stand-alone with strong scaffolding (3 tips + reference
  photo).
- Self-care emerging: brush teeth solo, dress self.
- Identity: "hero" framing lands hardest here.
- Parent's job: witness specifically, override warmly.

9-12 (late elementary / tween) — v0's sweet spot
- Kid surface: current build is tuned for this band.
- Chores: menu of options, picks own order.
- Risk: kid feels surveilled. Frame opt-in clearly.
- Override messages from parent: land with full weight.

13-15 (early teen)
- Kid surface: needs a voice shift. "Hero" and bunny read as babyish.
- Solution shipped: kid_mode='teen' on the kid record, same
  scaffolding with peer/coach voice and no mascot.
- Chores: household-shared and personal responsibilities.
- Tension: wants autonomy and validation.
- Parent's job: less direction, more witnessing.

16-18 (older teen)
- Kid surface: barely a chore app. More like a co-piloted life-skills
  tracker.
- Solution shipped: kid_mode='peer', flattest voice, household-OS
  framing.
- Chores: household contribution plus life-skill rehearsal.
- The clock: days-until-launch framing hits hardest here.
- Parent's job: consultant, not manager.
